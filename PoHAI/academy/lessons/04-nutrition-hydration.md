# Lesson 4 — Nutrition and hydration

Most "mystery" health problems in managed cattle are management problems in
disguise — usually diet balance, water, or transition timing.

## Water: the forgotten nutrient

A lactating cow drinks 50–100 liters a day. Water shortage shows up *before*
you'd expect: milk drop, constipation, reduced feed intake, and — critically —
rumen slowdown. Check flow rate and cleanliness, not just presence: a trough
exists but dribbles, or freezes, or sits full of algae, and the herd drinks
less than it needs. In heat, cows drink most at dusk and night — water must
be available then too.

## Rumen-first thinking

The rumen is a fermentation vat hosting billions of microbes. Everything you
feed is really feeding them, and they repay you with volatile fatty acids —
the cow's main energy source — plus microbial protein.

- **Fiber is not filler.** Roughage produces saliva (buffering the rumen) and
  keeps the rumen mat floating, which drives cud-chewing. Too little fiber +
  too much starch = subacute rumen acidosis: fewer cud chews, frothy or
  loose manure, poor production, eventually laminitis (hoof inflammation —
  see how the lessons connect).
- **Transition gradually.** Changing feed abruptly shocks the microbial
  population. Move to new rations over 1–3 weeks, especially into and out of
  lactation.
- **Balance the ratio, don't chase one number.** Forage-to-concentrate ratio
  matters more than any single ingredient. Fresh grass, quality hay, and a
  measured amount of grain is the classic shape; more grain is not "more
  care".

## Body condition

Score body condition regularly (1–5 scale, by feel of the hooks and pins —
visual-only scoring misjudges coat-covered animals). Thin cows calve poorly
and get sick; fat cows calve with difficulty and get ketosis. Steady beats
extreme: a cow should neither collapse in milk nor waddle.

## Mineral watchpoints

- **Calcium/phosphorus**: wrong ratio (especially low Ca relative to P around
  calving) causes milk fever (hypocalcemia) — the cow can't stand shortly
  after calving. This is a genuine emergency with real mortality.
- **Selenium/vitamin E**: deficiency (common in some regions' soils) shows up
  as white muscle disease in calves and weak immunity. Soil-test before
  supplementing — selenium has a narrow safe range.
- **Salt**: free-choice clean salt, always. Simple, cheap, non-negotiable.

## Evidence habits for nutrition

Post ration changes, body condition photos, and water checks as evidence.
"Changed hay supplier, watched intake for 3 days, no drop" is exactly the
kind of documentation that earns oracle credibility — it's boring, dated,
specific, and verifiable.
