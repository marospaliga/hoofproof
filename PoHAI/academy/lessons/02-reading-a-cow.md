# Lesson 2 — Reading a cow: early illness signs

Cows hide illness until it is advanced. The whole point of early reading is
to catch what the animal will not show you.

## Watch the herd, not just the individual

A healthy cow is in the middle of the herd, chewing cud while lying down,
eating eagerly at feed time. Isolation is a late and dangerous sign — by the
time a cow stands alone, the problem has been running for days.

## Daily signs worth recording (and posting as evidence)

- **Eating**: first to the trough, or last? A cow that skips a meal is
  telling you something. Reduced feed intake is the earliest measurable sign
  of most problems.
- **Rumination**: a healthy cow chews cud 40–60 minutes per hour while
  resting. Fewer than 30 chews per minute, or stopping entirely, means
  digestive trouble — often (subacute) rumen acidosis from too much grain or
  too little fiber.
- **Manure**: consistency and color. Loose, frothy, or significantly
  changed manure is an early alarm. This feels embarrassing to document, but
  it is some of the most valuable evidence an oracle can record.
- **Eyes**: bright, clear, moist. Sunken or dull eyes suggest dehydration or
  systemic illness. The sunken-eye test (pinch the skin above the eye; it
  should snap back in under 2 seconds) is a simple dehydration check.
- **Udder** (milking cows): heat, hardness, pain on touch, or streaks in
  milk mean mastitis — check with a strip cup, not by eye alone.
- **Hooves and gait**: lameness. Watch the cow walk on a hard surface. Head
  bobbing while walking, reluctance to bear weight, or an arched back while
  standing = foot pain. Count lameness in the herd — it is the most
  under-reported welfare problem in cattle.
- **Breathing**: 20–30 breaths per minute at rest. Faster, with the tail
  out and head extended, means respiratory distress — pneumonia is a top
  killer of young stock.

## What to do with a sign

1. Record it — time, cow, sign, photo if possible. Post it as evidence on
   the cow's page so backers and oracles see the same data you do.
2. Isolate if contagious signs are suspected (scours, respiratory).
3. Call a vet for: not eating for 24h, >40°C fever, inability to stand,
   calving complications, any sudden severe change. Knowing the *limits* of
   your competence is part of competence.
