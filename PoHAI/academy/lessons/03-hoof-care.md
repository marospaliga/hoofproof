# Lesson 3 — Hoof care basics

Lameness is the most expensive ignored problem in cattle: a lame cow eats
less, produces less, breeds poorly, and suffers daily. Hoof care is protection
work in the most literal sense.

## The structure, briefly

The hoof is two toes, each with a hard outer wall (like a thick fingernail),
a soft sole between and behind, and a heel. Weight rides mostly on the wall
and heel. Overgrowth, injury, or infection shifts weight onto soft tissue —
which is not built for it. That is lameness, mechanistically.

## Prevention

- **Flooring**: concrete without rest causes most chronic foot problems. Soft
  resting areas, clean dry bedding, and time off hard surfaces matter more
  than any treatment.
- **Hygiene**: wet, manure-soiled feet soften the skin between the toes and
  invite digital dermatitis (hairy heel wart) — the painful, bleeding
  lesions behind the heel. Keep standing areas clean and dry; footbaths with
  copper sulfate or formalin (handled with proper protection) reduce
  infectious pressure.
- **Trimming**: a functional trim evens the weight-bearing surfaces and
  removes overgrown wall. Routine trimming of the whole herd on a schedule
  (commonly 6–12 months, sooner for high-yield dairy) beats emergency
  individual trims.
- **Minerals**: zinc and biotin support hoof horn quality. Deficiency shows
  as weak, brittle, ridged horn months later.

## Common problems you should be able to name

- **Digital dermatitis**: raw, painful, bleeding lesion at the heel. Contagious
  through wet surfaces. Treat and fix the environment, or it comes back.
- **Foot rot**: sudden bilateral lameness with swelling between the toes and
  a foul smell — a *symmetrical* infection of the soft tissue. Different from
  a sole ulcer, which is one foot, one spot.
- **Sole ulcer**: a distinct lesion on the sole near the heel, often from
  poor trimming or prolonged standing. Visible as a bruised or open area.
- **Confusable pairs**: foot rot vs. digital dermatitis, sole ulcer vs.
  white line disease — if you can't distinguish two conditions, say so in
  your evidence. "Unsure between X and Y" is honest and useful; a confident
  wrong label is neither.

## When to call a professional

Deep lesions, a cow that cannot stand, swelling that spreads up the leg, or
any case that does not improve within days of treatment — a hoof trimmer or
veterinarian. A bad trim makes a lame cow permanently worse. Document
before-and-after with photos: this is exactly the kind of evidence raters
and oracles value.
