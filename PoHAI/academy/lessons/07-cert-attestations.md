# Certification lesson 7 — Labor attestations done right

Labor attestations are the bridge between real work and reputation: an
attested hour is Stasis, and more on well-rated cows. Because they mint
reputation, they attract abuse — vague hours, inflated work, friends
attesting each other for nothing. This lesson teaches attestations the way
an honest participant does them.

## What an attestation actually claims

An Attestation claim on HoofProof says: *this worker did these hours of
concrete work on this cow, and two attestors stake their own reputation on
it.* The claim carries a bond. If the claim is fraudulent, the bond is at
risk and the attestors' reputations are on the line — which is why
attestations need a quorum of distinct attestors, never a solo rubber stamp.

## Anatomy of a good claim

- **Worker named correctly**: the claim's subject is the *worker*, not the
  claimer. Credit flows to the hands that did the work.
- **Hours you would defend**: "4 hours" means four hours you could describe
  in detail if challenged — mucking out stalls 07:00–11:00, two people, ~30
  wheelbarrows. If you can't defend the number, cut it.
- **Work that is protection work**: feeding, mucking, hoof trimming, vet
  visits, fence repair, water systems, pasture work. Hours spent arguing on
  a forum are not labor hours.
- **Corroborating evidence attached**: photos with timestamps, receipts for
  materials, a second person's post describing the same workday. The
  attestation is the skeleton; evidence is the flesh.

## Attesting someone else honestly

When you attest another person's claim, you are staking your reputation on
their number. The honest protocol:

1. **You saw the work, or you verified it.** "I saw it" — you were there.
   "I verified it" — you checked evidence sufficient to vouch. If neither,
   don't attest; ask for evidence first. Declining to attest costs you
   nothing. Attesting fraudulently costs you everything.
2. **Check the hours against the evidence, not the friendship.** A refuted
   claim destroys the bonds behind it: the Stasis is slashed out of your
   score, not moved somewhere else. Your reputation carries that loss — but
   only if you actually checked before you signed.
3. **Never attest the same work twice** through different claims. Padding a
  2-hour job into two 2-hour claims is fraud by any name.

## Self-attestation and first entry

You can file a claim on your own work — but it still needs a quorum of
*other* attestors before it executes. There is no solo path from work to
reputation, by design. This is also why the course comes first: your first
attestation will be easier to vouch for if your quiz record already shows
you know what the work is.

## When claims go wrong

- A claim you attested turns out inflated: your bond and rating were at
  stake — this is the cost that keeps attestations honest.
- You see a fraudulent claim executing: rate the competing evidence, and if
  it's real fraud, the moderation ladder (propose → challenge → execute,
  decided by the reputation of the people who challenge it) exists for
  exactly this.
- Disputes about hours between worker and claimer: resolve with evidence
  before the quorum closes. An honest attestor declining is how disputes
  get caught early.

## The reputation loop, completed

Course → first Stasis → attest labor on real work → Stasis scaled by the
cow's rating → a bigger reputation, which is what lets you stake more, bond
bigger claims and carry more weight when you rate something. Every step of
that loop is protection work paying for itself. That is the whole economy —
and you now know how to participate in it honestly, which is the only way it
works.

   - c) A third person also says it's fine, without evidence
